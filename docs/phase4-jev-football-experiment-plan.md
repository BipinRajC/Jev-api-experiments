# Phase 4 — Jev experiments on real football matches

Plan date: 2026-10-18. Execution begins after the 2014–2019 FotMob backfill and the data gates below. This document defines the evaluation protocol; it does not report football performance.

## 1. Research question and scope

Measure which Jev primitive—**Noul, Score, or Choice**—gives the most useful forecasts across football markets, and whether those forecasts add information beyond mathematical models, tabular ML, and available pre-match prices. Preserve the same match-level evidence so a later FootyQuant ML model can be compared with Jev and, if justified, use frozen Jev outputs as additional inputs. No primitive is declared the winner from the earlier synthetic experiments.

The target competitions are Premier League, La Liga, Bundesliga, Serie A, Ligue 1, and UEFA Champions League. The first retrospective emphasis is **2025/26**, followed by the already played part of **2026/27**. Matches predicted and archived before kickoff later in 2026/27 form the decisive prospective cohort. Older seasons train and validate classical baselines; they are not the headline Jev result.

Every result is a 90-minute result including stoppage time, excluding extra time and penalties. Exclude cancelled, abandoned, awarded, disputed, and missing-score matches from scored outcomes. For event-count markets, verify whether the source includes extra time and exclude or reconstruct those matches accordingly. Report exclusions and missing data by competition, season, source, and market.

### Market inventory and initial priority

| Market family | Initial event or line | Outcome evidence in the current derived data | Market-price evidence in the current derived data | Plan |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1X2 | Home / draw / away after 90 minutes | `matches.goals_90_home/away` | Domestic football-data 1X2; no joined historical UCL odds | Core three-way test of every primitive. |
| Total goals | Over / under 2.5 after 90 minutes | Same 90-minute goals | Domestic football-data O/U 2.5; prospective quotes need capture | Core binary test of every primitive. |
| Both teams to score | Yes / no after 90 minutes | Same 90-minute goals | No BTTS price columns in the current joined odds table | Core outcome test; odds comparison only after a pre-cutoff quote is archived. |
| Total corners | A fixed half-corner line selected from historical development data, plus any separately logged bookmaker line | Domestic football-data `HC+AC` is present for 21,884 of 21,886 joined matches; FotMob has corners for both teams in all 30,222 currently parsed team-stat rows | No corner price columns in the current joined odds table | Core outcome test where a valid 90-minute corner label exists; odds comparison only with a matching line and pre-cutoff quote. |
| Candidate extensions | Team goals, first-half goals, handicap, shots on target, cards, or other markets supported by the incoming mathematical research | Goals, first-half scores, shots, and cards exist in varying source tables; coverage and settlement rules need a dedicated audit | Some handicap odds exist; the other candidate prices are not yet joined | Screen on training data, then register a smaller confirmation set before testing it prospectively. |

These counts describe the **current, partly rebuilt** Parquet files; remeasure after the active backfill finishes. The incoming mathematical research will shape the count models, priors, candidate lines, and later staking analysis. It will not be used to redefine a prospective prediction after its result is known. “Low variance” will be measured as repeat-call stability and uncertainty of the estimated performance, alongside calibration and predictive skill. A nearly constant forecast can have low output variance while being uninformative, so variance alone cannot select a market.

## 2. Three evaluation cohorts

| Cohort | Matches | Use | Interpretation |
|---|---|---|---|
| Historical development | 2014/15–2024/25 | Fit league frequencies, Elo-to-1X2 mapping, Poisson/Dixon–Coles and tabular ML; use rolling season validation | Model development only. Historical Pinnacle closing odds are an after-the-fact reference. |
| Retrospective Jev | 2025/26 plus 2026/27 matches already played before the first frozen run | Pilot, contamination probes, prompt and state ablations, exploratory comparison | These calls occur after results exist. Jev may have prior knowledge of them, so they cannot establish a live edge. |
| Prospective Jev | 2026/27 fixtures whose state, request, response, and available odds are saved **before kickoff** | Locked primary evaluation of primitives versus baselines and any matching same-cutoff market | This is the first cohort that can support a claim about real pre-match prediction. Keep UCL results separate from domestic leagues. |

Assign each match to a cohort by the **first prediction's recorded time**, not by season alone. A match predicted after kickoff stays retrospective even if its state was reconstructed from earlier data. Never move a match between cohorts after seeing its result.

## 3. Gates before any football Jev batch

1. Finish the 2014–2019 FotMob match-detail backfill. Compare every finished target fixture against its expected raw detail path; parse and validate the saved bodies. Rebuild `data/derived/*.parquet` only after the backfill finishes, then record row counts, null rates, source hashes, and the build commit.
2. Fix the source join in `src/data/tables.py`: competition, season, and home/away IDs are insufficient when clubs meet twice with the same home side. The 2022/23 Spezia–Verona league game and playoff are the known counterexample. Include kickoff date and score checks, require an unambiguous candidate, and re-audit the five currently flagged score conflicts. Keep football-data's `HxG` and `AxG` out of odds columns.
3. Make every collector return a failing exit status when any fetch fails. Verify that the daily job and data audit distinguish a complete refresh from a partial one. Correct the snapshot count in `status`.
4. Audit FotMob versus football-data kickoff times where both exist, especially differences over 15 minutes. Define one adjudicated kickoff per match and log the source. Resolve or exclude discrepancies that could change the information cutoff.
5. Add an **upcoming-fixture query path** to the as-of engine. The current `features.parquet` contains only finished matches. A prospective state must be built from an unplayed fixture at its recorded cutoff without using that fixture's result or completed-match record.
6. Add a near-cutoff capture job for forward quotes and fixtures. The current daily snapshot only stores raw responses and cannot guarantee a price near a match's cutoff. For the primary prospective protocol, assemble the state and capture the available quote starting 75 minutes before kickoff; complete the Jev call no later than 60 minutes before kickoff. Require a quote captured within 15 minutes before the Jev request for a same-cutoff market comparison. If no quote qualifies, retain the Jev prediction and mark the market comparison missing.
7. Prove field availability, not only event ordering. Historical completed-match lineups have no release timestamp in this cache: omit actual XI and lineup-derived features from the 60-minute state unless a timestamped pre-cutoff snapshot proves they were available. Exclude post-match stats, final tables, labels, and closing odds from every state.
8. Build a market-label audit with source, unit, period, and settlement rule for every fixture. Check O/U and BTTS labels against 90-minute goals, corner totals across football-data and FotMob where both exist, and whether FotMob `Periods.All` includes extra time. A corner label without confirmed 90-minute scope is excluded. Audit candidate-market coverage before adding any other market.
9. Parse and validate each market quote by bookmaker, line, side, decimal price, capture time, and exact settlement rule. A quote for O/U 9.5 corners cannot evaluate an O/U 10.5 forecast. Keep outcome-only evaluation when no matching price exists.
10. Freeze the state schema, question wording, primitive-specific probability extraction, model ID, candidate lines, cohort assignment, exclusions, metrics, and comparison arms **before** the scored batch. A small pilot may test parsing and cost, but its outcomes cannot select the winning prompt or market for the locked evaluation.

The data gates are checked again after any source or parser change. The real-data future-scramble test remains useful, but it does not replace proof that a field was published before the cutoff.

## 4. Matched Jev primitive protocol and archived record

Use one structured JSON state per fixture. It contains match identity, competition, kickoff and cutoff UTC, source provenance, domestic standing when known, recent results and goals, cross-league Elo, rolling shots and xG, rest/congestion from competitions actually covered, and explicit coverage flags. Use stable key order and units. Omit unknown measurements instead of fabricating values. The default state contains **no bookmaker prices**.

On the **same fixtures and same frozen state**, ask Noul, Score, and Choice semantically matched questions for each registered event. Keep the 90-minute period and line in the instruction itself. Use a fixed question-set version and seeded rotation of primitive request order. Batch the three 1X2 propositions within each primitive where the API allows it. Store request count, input tokens, latency, failures, and repeat-call spread along with accuracy; the three output types have different cost and response shapes. The [TypeSafe API reference](https://docs.typesafe.ai/api) defines their current request and response forms.

Initial canonical event wording, with its proposition reused across primitive-specific instructions: “The home team wins after 90 minutes plus stoppage time, excluding extra time and penalties”; likewise for draw and away win; “The match has at least 3 total goals after 90 minutes plus stoppage time”; “Both teams score at least one goal after 90 minutes plus stoppage time”; and “The match has more than `{corner_line}` total corners during 90 minutes plus stoppage time.” The actual corner line is stored as a parameter. These templates, their exact punctuation, and the Score/Choice criteria are frozen in the first question-set artifact before scored calls.

| Primitive | Binary event such as O/U 2.5, BTTS, or corners O/U | 1X2 output converted to probabilities |
|---|---|---|
| Noul | One literal yes/no question; `noul` is the raw event probability | Ask home win, draw, and away win separately; store all three raw values and normalize their sum for the primary 1X2 distribution. |
| Score | Ask how likely the same event is using **nine explicitly labelled probability levels** from 0% to 100% in 12.5-point steps; convert returned `score / 8` to a raw event probability | Ask three parallel probability-rubric questions; store raw values and normalize their sum. Also retain the rubric-level probability distribution. |
| Choice | Ask yes/no with fixed criteria; use `probabilities["yes"]` as the raw event probability | Primary Choice arm asks home/draw/away in one question and uses its returned distribution. A separately labelled one-vs-rest Choice arm checks whether the three binary choices behave differently. |

The exact text, criteria, rubric, response keys, and line selection are versioned in the experiment artifact before scored calls. For every primitive, reject and log non-finite, out-of-range, missing, or incoherent outputs; a zero-sum set cannot be normalized. Keep raw and normalized 1X2 values separate. Never substitute Choice's `confidence` or Score's `confidence` for an event probability. Fit any post-hoc calibration map on earlier development predictions only, then evaluate raw and calibrated outputs on the same untouched fixtures.

Add a **count-band arm** for total goals and total corners after their label rules are audited: Score uses an ordinal rubric of at most ten goal/corner bands; Choice selects among the same bands; Noul asks the corresponding registered over/under threshold directly. The bands must align with the tested threshold so a probability above that threshold can be derived from the full Score/Choice band distribution. Evaluate the band forecasts as distributions as well as their derived over/under probability. This tests whether Score or Choice is more effective in its native structured form, not just as a probability rubric. Lock bins using historical development matches before scoring 2025/26 or prospective fixtures.

Pin `jev-1.13.0` for the first full arm to preserve continuity with Experiments 001–019. TypeSafe's [model reference](https://docs.typesafe.ai/models) now also lists `jev-1.14.0` and maps `jev-preview` to it; run a separately named, paired 1.14 pilot for compatibility and cost, then predeclare whether to run both versions prospectively. Do not select a version by retrospective score or use moving aliases in archived requests. The [API reference](https://docs.typesafe.ai/api) confirms the `state`/`model`/`questions` request shape and token-usage response; verify account access and limits at execution time.

For every call, save an immutable record under `results/020-football/` or its numbered successor: match, market, line, primitive, and cohort IDs; UTC request/response/cutoff/kickoff times; raw state and its SHA-256; question-set version; code/data versions; model requested/returned; raw response; request ID; token usage; parsing result; and any error. For prospective calls, also save the chosen odds quote, bookmaker, market, line, capture timestamp, and raw snapshot hash. Write each prospective prediction record **before its cutoff**, then append the eventual result later without rewriting the prediction. Retrospective calls are labelled with their actual, post-match request time. Never store API keys.

## 6. Benchmarks, splits, and statistics

Primary probability metric: **three-class Brier score** for 1X2, `mean((pH-yH)^2 + (pD-yD)^2 + (pA-yA)^2)`, and **binary Brier score**, `mean((p-y)^2)`, for O/U, BTTS, and corners. Lower is better. Compare primitives **within the same market and the same matches**, then report Brier skill relative to that market's prior-season baseline; a 1X2 Brier and a binary Brier are not directly comparable. Secondary metrics: fixed-floor log loss, calibration/reliability by outcome and probability bin, count-band ranked probability score, coverage/failure rate, input tokens, latency, and repeat-call standard deviation. Report 95% uncertainty intervals for paired score differences by resampling match rounds or dates, with league and season breakdowns. Predeclare Noul-versus-Score and Noul-versus-Choice as the two primitive contrasts in each core market; adjust or clearly label the resulting multiple comparisons before claiming a winner.

Build baselines in increasing complexity: prior-season event frequencies for every market; the repository's as-of Elo mapped to H/D/A; a joint-goals Poisson/Dixon–Coles model for 1X2, O/U and BTTS; a corner-count model selected after a historical dispersion check; then market-specific tabular ML using only admissible as-of fields. For additional markets, select a mathematical baseline and settlement rule before evaluating Jev on them. Fit/tune with rolling-origin seasons through 2024/25, use 2025/26 for retrospective validation, and keep the prospective series sealed. For a Jev stack, generate time-respecting Jev inputs and out-of-fold base-model predictions; never train on a row's own target-derived or closing-odds fields.

For 2025/26, football-data's [published warning](https://football-data.co.uk/data) says its Pinnacle feed became unreliable after 23 July 2025. Do not call those prices a clean sharp-market benchmark. Use available market-average/Betfair 1X2 and O/U 2.5 closing data as **post-hoc references only**, with source and missingness reported. The current joined odds have no BTTS or corners prices, and historical UCL has no joined odds, so those retrospective comparisons are against outcomes and non-market models. In the prospective cohort, compare with a bookmaker quote only if its line and settlement match the question and its recorded capture time is at or before the prediction cutoff; a later closing quote is a separate diagnostic, never an input or a same-cutoff comparator.

For each market, report paired Brier differences between Jev primitives, the corresponding mathematical baseline, and a same-cutoff market quote on the common-coverage subset. Also report Jev plus ML versus ML alone. A market with lower result variance, a steadier Jev output, or one profitable-looking subset is not automatically the best betting market: require good calibration, improvement over its own baseline, sufficient matched fixtures, a usable quoted price, and uncertainty narrow enough to support the conclusion. Treat the many ablation, market-extension, and subgroup results as exploratory unless an untouched prospective cohort replicates them. First prospective interim report: after 250 eligible completed matches, descriptive only. Final 2026/27 report: after the season ends, including every eligible prediction and all failures.

## 7. Operating and decision rules

- Before paying for a full batch, check current Jev balance and organization limits, then measure input tokens and errors on the 30–50-match pilot. TypeSafe currently lists both pinned versions at [$0.042 per million input tokens](https://docs.typesafe.ai/models); use measured usage, not the transcript's estimated tokens per match, to set a dollar cap.
- Freeze a manifest of eligible fixtures before each batch. Log missing states, unavailable prices, timeouts, and invalid outputs as failures; do not replace them with easier fixtures. Do not rerun a failed prediction after kickoff and label it prospective.
- Preserve raw evidence and exact exclusion reasons. Compare source revisions against the snapshot hash. A changed historical source may prompt a sensitivity rerun, but the original prediction stays immutable.
- Decide which primitive works best **per market** from paired prospective skill, calibration, repeatability, and cost; retain all three raw outputs even if one is later selected for a product. Decide whether Jev adds value to FootyQuant from incremental prospective skill over the fixed ML model and available same-cutoff market, not from synthetic calibration or retrospective ROI. Treat outcome-only markets as prediction research until usable prices exist.
